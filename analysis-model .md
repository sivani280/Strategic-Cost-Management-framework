## Case Definiation
A manufacturing company depends heavily on a single supplier for a critical production material.The supplier continuously increase the material price,resulting in
increasing manufacturing costs and pressure on product margins
## Initial Questions
How dependent is the company on the primary supplier?
How does a supplier price increase affect material cost?
How does the material cost change affect manufacturing cost?
What happens if the company introduces another supplier?
What cost or risk trade-offs arise from supplier diversification?
## Problem Statement
The company is exposed to increading manufacturing costs because it relies heavily on a sigle supplier for a critical production material.
Continous increases in the supplier's material proce can reduce the company's product margin and increase its overall cost structure
The analysis will investigate how supplier dependency affects cost and whether introducing alternative suppliers can reduce cost and supply risk
## Objective
The objective of this analysis to evaluate the impact of supplier dependency and material price increases on manufacturing costs and product margins.
The analysis will also evaluate supplier diversification as a potential strategy for reducing cost exposure and supply risk, while considering the additional costs and risks associated with using alternative suppliets

## Factor Relationships
The analysis will examine how the changes in supplier and material-related factors affect the company's overall cost structure
### Supplier Price Impact
An increase in the material price charged by the primary supplier is expected to increase the company's material cost, assuming the quantity purchased remains unchanged.
### Manufacturing Cost Impact
An increase in material cost is expected to increase total manufacturing cost when other manufacturing costs remain unchanged.
### Margin Impact
An increase in manufacturing cost can reduce the product margin if the selling price reamins unchanged
### Supplier Diversification Impact
Introducing an alternative supplier can reduce the company's dependency on the primary supplier.
However, diversification may introduce additional costs and operational risks.
The analysis will therefore consider both the potential benefits and trade-offs of supplier diversification rather than assuming that diversifaction is automatically beneficial.
## Basic Calculations
The following calculations will be used to measure the effect of supplier price changes on the company's costand products margin
### Material Cost
Material Cost = Material Price * Material Quantity
### Total Manufacturing Cost
Total manufacturing cost will be calculated as:
Total Manufacturing Cost = Material Cost + Other Manufacturing Costs
### Product Margin
Product margin will be calculated as:
Product Marginc= Selling Price - Total Manfacturing Cost

# Supplier Scenarios
The company currently depends heavily on a  primary supplier fro the critical aluminium ally material.
To evaluate the effect of supplier diversification, different sourcing scenarios will  be compared.
## Scenario 1 - Single Supplier
The company sources the enitre material requirement from the primary supplier.
This represents the current supplier-dependent situation and will be used as the baseline scenario.
## Scenario 2 - Partial Diversification
The company continues ourchasing most of its material from the primary supplier while the sourcing a smaller portion from a secondary supplier.
This scenario is intended to resuce supplier depencdency while maintaining a significant relationship with the primary supplier.
## Scenario 3 - Balanced Diversification
The material requirement is divided more evenly between primary and secondary suppliers.
This scenario provides a greater reduction in dependency but may introduce additional coordination and operational costs.
## Scenario 4 - High Diversification
The company sources most of its material requirement from alternative suppliers and significantly reduces its dependence on the primary supplier.
This scenario may reduce supplier concentration risk but could introduce additional costs related to supplier qualification,qualitu control,logistics, and coordination.
